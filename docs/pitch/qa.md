# Judge Q&A sheet

Questions a local, NASA or Space Apps judge is likely to ask after the 2:42 talk. Each answer is at most two spoken sentences. Say the number, name the source, stop. If you don't know, say so and say where the answer would come from. Never give a number that is not in the "Backed by" line.

Fill in "Who answers" before the talk.

## Questions

| # | Question | Answer | Backed by | Who answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where does your data come from, and how accurate is the terrain? | The terrain is NASA's HiRISE elevation model of Jezero, resampled to a 2 m grid, with HiRISE, CTX and THEMIS images over it. Against the same model on NASA Mars Trek our grid differs by 0.14 m RMS over 564,001 points, which checks our processing, not HiRISE itself. | `docs/data/mars-trek-check/README.md`; the `credits` beat in `src/present/beats.js` | |
| 2 | How does the route planner decide a route? | It runs A* on the 2 m height grid, where each cell costs its distance times a slope and rock penalty, and ground over 20° is blocked. The crew's needs, its goal, crew size, time and risk, decide which stops it keeps and how much reserve it holds. | `src/sim/costGrid.js`, `src/plan/useRoutePlanner.js`, `src/sim/missionNeeds.js` (#282) | |
| 3 | How do you compute the return zone? | Two Dijkstra passes over the same grid: one from the habitat gives the walk home from every point, the other from the astronaut gives the walk out. A point is inside when the walk there, the work there and the walk home all fit the time, oxygen and battery the crew planned. | `src/sim/returnZone.js` (#335) | |
| 4 | Your radiation data is from Gale, not Jezero. Why? | No instrument has measured surface radiation at Jezero, so we replay the September 2017 solar event that Curiosity's RAD measured at Gale crater. The app says "Gale crater, not Jezero" next to every value. | `docs/report/REPORT.md` (Radiation alert); `public/data/hazards.json` | |
| 5 | What is real and what is simulated? | Terrain, images, weather, radiation dose rates and the suit limits are NASA data; slope, routes and oxygen per metre are derived from them; walking speed and when events arrive are our assumptions. Every number on screen carries a DATA, DERIVED or SIM tag that names its source. | `docs/VALIDITY.md`; `src/data/provenance.js` | |
| 6 | Who would use this, and when? | Nobody outside the team has used it yet. We built it for crews to plan and rehearse a walk before launch and before each walk on the surface, and the visor shows what the same numbers would look like during the walk. | `docs/CHALLENGE_FIT.md` | |
| 7 | How did you validate the suit and oxygen model? | Oxygen per metre comes from Minetti's published cost of walking on slopes, scaled to Mars gravity and calibrated to NASA's suited walking tests by Norcross and colleagues in 2008 and 2010. It does not model a Mars suit's own stiffness, and the tank it uses is from the xEMU, a lunar suit design. | `src/sim/suitModel.js`; `docs/report/REPORT.md` (Suit model) | |
| 8 | How does EV2, the crewmate, decide what to do? | EV2 is a scripted state machine, not a second player: it follows the route behind you, waits at stops, closes in during a dust storm, leads to the shelter in a radiation alert and turns home when its own margin runs low. A real second player online lives on the `feat/online` branches and is not on main. | `src/sim/crewmate.js`; `docs/PARTNER.md` | |
| 9 | What about the communication delay to Earth? | We don't model it, and Marswalk has no Earth link. NASA's BASALT field tests ran with 5 and 15 minute one-way delays, which is why the margin sits on the crew's visor and not in mission control. | `docs/CHALLENGE_FIT.md`; `docs/report/REPORT.md` (limits) | |
| 10 | How did you use AI tools? | AI coding agents wrote most of the code from our own issues, and AI research agents found the NASA papers behind our numbers; a teammate other than the author reviewed 38 of the 134 pull requests merged by 15 November. No AI model runs inside the app, and the only AI-generated images, the start video and three hazard illustrations, are labelled as AI. | `docs/SUBMISSION.md` (Use of Super Intelligence) | |
| 11 | What are the biggest limitations? | One site, radiation and storm data from Gale rather than Jezero, a suit model built on lunar suit tests, and event timing that is our own. An audit traced 109 numbers to their sources and found 2 wrong on screen, and each one has an issue. | `docs/VALIDITY.md`; see "Hard questions" below | |
| 12 | What would you build next? | Planning the walk around sampling tasks, what to collect, where and how many, is keiwv's open PR #358, and making every screen usable on a phone is in progress in #371. After that, online crews from the `feat/online` branches, and other sites with a HiRISE terrain model. | PR #358, PR #371, branch `feat/online` | |
| 13 | Why Jezero and Cheyava Falls? | Jezero is where Perseverance works, so NASA has a HiRISE terrain model, rover drives, weather and photos for the same ground. Cheyava Falls is the rock where Perseverance found a potential biosignature, unconfirmed, which gives the crew a reason to walk. | `docs/report/REPORT.md`; Hurowitz et al. 2025, Nature | |
| 14 | Which kinds of data does it combine? The challenge asks for three or more. | Four: HiRISE terrain and orbital images, Curiosity's radiation readings and 2018 storm opacity, and Perseverance's MEDA weather, Mastcam-Z images and drives. Each one changes something on screen: the route, the alerts, the weather readout or the science stop. | `docs/CHALLENGE_FIT.md`; `docs/credits.md` | |
| 15 | Does it run on a phone or a tablet? | It runs in a desktop browser with WebGL today. Making every screen usable on a phone is PR #371, in progress and not on main. | PR #371 | |
| 16 | How do you navigate without GPS? | The app keeps the true position apart from the estimate: walking adds error, and stopping with a clear horizon models a terrain fix that cuts it. That is our model, tagged SIM, and its numbers are not yet sourced. | `src/sim/navigation.js`, `src/sim/navigationDrift.js`; `docs/VALIDITY.md` | |
| 17 | Is the dust storm real? | The opacity is: it rises from the clear-sky value Mastcam-Z measured at Jezero to the peak of the 2018 global storm Curiosity measured at Gale. How fast it rises is our assumption, tagged SIM. | `public/data/hazards.json`; `docs/report/REPORT.md` (Dust storm) | |
| 18 | Why a return zone? Why not just show the oxygen left? | Oxygen left does not tell you whether you can get home; the walk back does, and Apollo planned the same way, with a padded walkback estimate and a 30 minute reserve. The zone shows that answer as an area on the map. | `docs/report/REPORT.md`; Norcross et al. 2009, NASA/TP-2009-214796 | |
| 19 | How is this different from NASA Mars Trek? | Mars Trek shows the terrain. Marswalk plans a walk on that terrain, prices it in time and oxygen, and shows where the crew can still get home. | `docs/CHALLENGE_FIT.md` | |
| 20 | Does it use live data from Mars? | No. Every dataset is a stored NASA product, and the alerts replay events that missions measured. | `docs/report/REPORT.md` (limits) | |
| 21 | What did you build during the hackathon, and what existed before? | (Team to confirm before saying it.) The repo was created empty on 14 November at 8:47 am, 13 minutes before the start, and the app was built during the event. The terrain tile, astronaut, habitat and test bench were made on 12 and 13 November, and `docs/RULES.md` asks the team to tell the Ensenada lead and ask whether the project stays eligible. | `docs/RULES.md` (PR #315) | |

## Hard questions

Limits a judge may press on. Each answer admits the limit first, then says what we did about it.

| Question | Answer | Backed by | Who answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| The radiation is from Gale, not Jezero. Isn't that wrong for Jezero? | It is a real surface measurement from another crater, because nobody has measured radiation on the ground at Jezero. We label it on screen and never call it Jezero data. | `docs/report/REPORT.md`; `public/data/hazards.json` | |
| Your suit model is simplified. | Yes: it prices walking on slopes from published tests and NASA's lunar suit data, and it does not model joint stiffness, temperature or a Mars-specific suit. It is tagged DERIVED, and the sources are on the Sources screen. | `src/sim/suitModel.js`; `docs/report/REPORT.md` (Suit model) | |
| It only works at one site. | Today it has one tile of Jezero. Nothing in the planner or the zone depends on Jezero, so any site with a HiRISE terrain model could be added, but we have not done it. | `src/sim/costGrid.js`; `docs/report/REPORT.md` | |
| The event timing is made up. | The timing is ours and tagged SIM: we compress the 2017 radiation event and the storm's rise into minutes so they fit one walk. The dose rates and the storm's peak opacity are the measured ones. | `src/sim/missions.js`; `public/data/hazards.json` | |
| The dose on the visor is not what the real event delivered. | Correct: we keep the measured rates but squeeze 57 hours into minutes, so the total dose is smaller. We filed it as #295. | Issue #295 | |
| The margin reads differently on the visor and at the table. | Yes: the visor takes the smaller of the oxygen margin and the 8-hour clock, and the table shows the oxygen margin. It is a known issue, #323. | Issues #323 and #312 | |
| How much of this did AI write, and who checked it? | AI agents wrote most of the code from our issues, and we reviewed what they produced; 38 of the 134 merged pull requests had a review from a teammate other than the author. An audit run with AI agents and checked by us traced 109 numbers to their sources. | `docs/SUBMISSION.md` (Use of Super Intelligence); `docs/VALIDITY.md` | |
